# Concepts from the course slides (Start-up Launch, prof. de Jaegher, 2025/26) useful for the thesis

Source: lecture slides L02-L06 provided by the student (BEP analysis, pro-forma IS and BS, covering financial needs, market analysis, competitors analysis, value proposition). The WHO/WHAT/HOW/ECONOMIC MODEL framework itself is not in these decks (possibly in L01, not provided). The course builds on Parolini, C. (2021), *Business Planning: Turning an Idea into a Reality* (self-published, KDP; Italian original: *Business Planning. Dall'idea al progetto imprenditoriale*, Pearson Italia, 3rd ed. 2020).

Paraphrased, not copied. Use these to connect the thesis to the professor's own framework.

## 1. Business models and cost structure (L02 pro-forma IS; L05 competitors)
The slides distinguish three cost logics and the scalability they imply:
- direct and variable costs, typical of artisanal activities (poorly scalable);
- structural fixed costs, typical of industrial activities;
- development costs, typical of development-based activities (highly scalable: unit cost falls fast with quantity).

**Use in the thesis:** classic SaaS is a development-based, highly scalable model. Generative and agentic AI add a variable cost per use (inference), pushing part of the model back toward a less scalable cost structure. This explains AI credits, caps on free AI usage and cost-driven price changes (strategy 5).

## 2. Pricing logics (L06 value proposition)
Prices can be set on costs (cost-based), on competitors (competition-based) or on willingness to pay (value-based). Price can and should be articulated.

**Use in the thesis:** the observed AI pricing looks largely cost-based (credits as cost caps) rather than value- or outcome-based. Directly supports the reformulated strategy 5.

## 3. Product/service system and value proposition (L06)
An offering has tangible elements, intangible elements, economic elements (price, conditions) and non-monetary costs (for example time to learn the product). The slides also ask how the product/service system could evolve, and stress value as perceived by customers and the customers' customers.

**Use in the thesis:** agentic AI cuts the non-monetary cost of learning and using the editor (the agent does the work), which changes the WHAT. Useful lens for strategy 1 (own agent).

## 4. Buying-centre roles (L04 market analysis)
Initiator, influencer, decision-maker, buyer, user, payer, gatekeeper.

**Use in the thesis:** when an AI agent operates the product, user and payer can separate (the agent uses, the human or company pays), and AI assistants such as ChatGPT or Claude can act as gatekeepers between the SaaS and the end customer. A precise way to describe changes in the WHO (strategies 2 and 3).

## 5. Competitive analysis (L05)
Barriers to entry and exit, potential competitors, substitutes, five (six) forces including complementary goods producers, strategic groups; competitor analysis also serves to innovate the business model.

**Use in the thesis:** matches the professor's framing in the call (falling entry barriers, new entrants). AI labs and general assistants can be at the same time substitutes (they generate the artifact) and complementors (they use the SaaS through MCP or apps). Gamma, Claude Design and Google Pics are new entrants.

## 6. Business Model Canvas, frontstage and backstage (L06)
The course uses the Business Model Canvas (key partners, key activities, key resources, value propositions, customer relationships, channels, customer segments, cost structure and financial needs, revenue model) and make, buy or connect choices for each activity.

**Use in the thesis:** WHO maps to customer segments, relationships and channels; WHAT to value propositions; HOW to partners, activities and resources (make, buy or connect: build an own agent, acquire an AI-native, or connect to external agents); ECONOMIC MODEL to revenue model and cost structure. "Make, buy or connect" is a neat way to frame strategies 1, 7 and 2-3.
