# Review report: Chapter 3, literature review (draft parts A and B)

Reviewer role: thesis committee member (strategic management and information systems). Files reviewed: WRITING_BRIEF.md, PROTOCOL.md, ch3_part_A.md, ch3_part_B.md; checked against the eight stream reviews, master_register.csv, academic_register.csv, grey_register.csv, grey_notes.md, note_slide_corso.md and the five case files. The draft files were not edited and no git command was run.

---

## 1. Overall assessment

The chapter is a serious and unusually candid piece of work. Its strengths are real: the evidence base is broad and mostly well labelled (peer-reviewed, preprint, practitioner), claims are generally hedged, the SaaSpocalypse typology (Table 3.1) is a genuine synthesis, the Bain "open door / battleground" reading in 3.6.3 corrects a common misquotation, and the cost-versus-value tests for credits in 3.7.4 are a usable analytical device. Fact-checking against the registers found most numbers correct (see Section 4: of 46 claims sampled, 33 are fully supported, 9 are supported with a qualification, 4 are wrong, overstated or not supported by the registered source). The em dash rule is respected (zero occurrences in either part).

However, the chapter is not yet submission-ready as one piece. (i) It does not open as a chapter: the research question and the seven strategies S1-S7 are never stated or defined before they are used, so the red thread exists only in the author's head. (ii) Backstage verification language ("known here only through summaries", "(general argument)", "I had access to summaries only", stream codes AG/SP/BM, "AI research agents") sits in the body text and would read to a committee as admitting that sources were not read. (iii) The register rules of the brief are broken in a few places (two check_first sources cited; several case-file sources not in the master register; a placeholder reference). (iv) The headline statistic of 3.1, "543 unique sources", is wrong: the register has 543 rows but about 490 distinct works. (v) The two reference lists cannot simply be concatenated: about 45 works appear in both, and several same-author same-year entries collide (Figma, Inc. 2026; Salesforce 2026; Teece 2018; Dell'Acqua 2023 versus 2026; Bain 2025 cited under two author forms). (vi) The body is about 14,000 words (about 6,900 in A and 7,100 in B) with roughly 300 distinct references, which is too much for a 25-30 page chapter and produces long citation chains where the review summarizes instead of weighing. (vii) A few statements are contradicted by the project's own evidence (Section 3, items M1-M9).

**Grade of readiness: B-minus / about 70% ready.** The content foundation is sound; one structured revision cycle (about 2 to 3 working days) is needed on structure, register compliance, reference merging, cuts of about 25% and the repairs listed below. After that it can go to the supervisor.

### CRITICAL

**C1. The chapter has no introduction, never states the research question, and uses S1-S7 before defining them.**
Location: start of 3.1 ("This chapter integrates eight stream reviews into one account...") and 3.2.6 ("It informs S1 through autonomy scales...", first use of S1 to S5). The words "research question" occur once in the whole chapter (3.10.3 heading); SQ3 never appears; L1-L4 are listed once in 3.1 and never used again; the seven strategies are described only in 3.9.1, and R-outcome / R-governance are defined only there and in 3.8.4.
Problem: A reader meeting "S4 is the core of the defence but also leaks the asset" in 3.5.6 does not know what S4 is. The argument chain (AI shifts the user from person to agent -> debate and its blind spot on creation tools -> theories of adaptation -> entry barriers and complementary assets -> platform terms -> pricing and cost -> what is special about creation software -> strategies -> gaps) is plausible but not announced, and the sections end with "implications" that point to strategies the reader cannot yet identify.
Fix: add an opening section "3.0 Purpose and structure" (about 350 words) before 3.1 and a compact definitional table. Suggested text:

> This chapter reviews the literature needed to answer the thesis research question: how are creation-oriented productivity SaaS incumbents evolving their business models as agentic AI enables digital artifacts to be produced by AI agents, both inside their products and through external agents that use them? The question has three parts: SQ1 asks which roles incumbents take toward agents, on which access terms and when; SQ2 asks what changes in the business model (who is served, what is offered, how it is delivered, and the economic model) and how deep the change is; SQ3 asks how value is captured, and at what cost of AI, when agents do the work. "Creation-oriented productivity SaaS" denotes subscription software whose core job is to help a user produce an editable artifact (a design, a deck, a document, a text); the cases are Figma, Canva, Adobe Express and Grammarly, with Gamma as AI-native benchmark. The review proceeds in five steps. Sections 3.2 and 3.3 establish what agentic AI is and what the SaaSpocalypse debate does and does not say about creation tools. Sections 3.4 and 3.5 supply the theories of adaptation, entry and complementary assets. Sections 3.6 to 3.8 treat the three domains in which incumbents act: platforms and access terms, pricing and cost, and creation software itself. Section 3.9 integrates these into seven candidate strategies and two rival hypotheses, and Section 3.10 states the gaps the thesis addresses.

Then add "Table 3.0. Candidate strategies and rival hypotheses" with seven rows: S1 own agent inside the product; S2 being used by external agents (MCP, apps in assistants, APIs) under different terms; S3 hosting third-party agents; S4 serving proprietary context (brand kits, design systems, data) to agents; S5 cost-driven hybrid monetization (seat anchor plus credits, rationed free AI); S6 expanding scope; S7 acquiring AI-native companies; plus R-outcome (outcome-based pricing) and R-governance (governance as a lever). Use the labels S1-S7 identically in every section (see M8). Keep L1-L4 only if each section ends by saying which L it answers; otherwise delete them from 3.1.
