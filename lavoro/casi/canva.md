## B. Brief history (about 220 words)

Canva was founded in 2012 by Melanie Perkins, Cliff Obrecht and Cameron Adams and launched its browser-based editor in 2013, with the promise of making design simple for people without professional training (press release, 10 Apr 2025; Wikipedia). Growth was product-led and freemium: templates, a drag-and-drop editor and a large stock library (Pixabay and Pexels were acquired in 2019). A team product followed in 2019 (first branded "Enterprise", in practice aimed at teams of 5 to 100) and Canva Teams in 2020 (TechCrunch, 23 May 2024; MarTech, 25 Sep 2024). Canva says it has been free-cash-flow profitable for about nine years, while ASIC accounts show statutory losses for 2022-2024 (Startup Daily, 6 May 2026). The valuation peaked at US$40bn in 2021 and was reset to US$26bn in 2022. In late 2022 the company passed 100 million monthly users, launched the Visual Worksuite (docs, whiteboards, websites) and its first generative feature, Magic Write. From 2023 it layered generative AI (Magic Studio), moved up-market (a real Enterprise plan in 2024) and began buying companies (Affinity, Leonardo.ai). In 2025-2026 it reframed itself as "an AI platform with a bunch of design tools" (Obrecht, TechCrunch, 18 Feb 2026), opened to ChatGPT and Claude, built proprietary models, and then hit an AI cost shock: a cut to 2026 growth guidance (Aug 2026) and a 22% workforce reduction (Nov 2026).

## C. Business model at t0 (mid-2022)

Most direct 2022 sources (pricing page, Canva newsroom) could not be opened in this session. The description below combines retrospective but verified press sources with items that the student must confirm on the Wayback snapshots listed at the end of this section.

### WHO (customer segments, channels, relationships)
- **Mass base of non-designers** (individuals, small businesses, educators, students, marketers): the platform was built for "users without professional design training or experience" (Wikipedia [V-open: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canva, accessed 2026-11-07], level C). Over 100m MAU by Oct 2022 [V-snippet: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20221012005414/en].
- **Small teams** as the paid growth engine: in 2019 Canva moved "from supporting mostly individual users to supporting small teams from between five and 100 users" (Cam Adams, TechCrunch 23 May 2024 [V-open: https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/23/canva-launches-a-proper-enterprise-product-and-they-mean-it-this-time/, accessed 2026-11-07]).
- **Enterprises only marginally:** the 2019 "enterprise product" was "in reality ... more geared toward teams than fulfilling true enterprise requirements" (same TechCrunch article). A true Enterprise plan came only in May 2024.
- **Education and nonprofits:** Canva for Education launched in Dec 2019 as a free product (Wikipedia [V-open], level C). Free nonprofit plan [M].
- **Channels:** self-serve web and mobile, SEO and search intent (Obrecht: "we drove a lot of early days in Canva through Google, really understood people's search intent, and delivered them content that they could then go into our product and edit", TechCrunch 18 Feb 2026 [V-open: https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/18/canva-gets-to-4b-in-revenue-as-llm-referral-traffic-rises/, accessed 2026-11-07]), word of mouth and team invitations.

### WHAT (value proposition)
- Easy, fast design without skills: templates replace the blank page, drag-and-drop replaces pixel editing ("It replaced intimidating blank pages with millions of templates, and manual, pixel-by-pixel editing with intuitive drag-and-drop creation", press release 16 Apr 2026 [V-open: https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/economia/business_wire/2026/04/16/canva-unveils-canva-ai-2.0-reimagining-how-the-world-designs-and_1dd656c5-d9a1-4be0-ba76-a1d840827b9e.html, accessed 2026-11-07], level A, retrospective).
- Artifacts: social graphics, presentations, posters, documents, short video, print products (Wikipedia [V-open], level C). The Visual Worksuite (Docs, Whiteboards, Websites) was launched in Sep 2022, just after t0 (press release 10 Apr 2025 refers to "the launch of Canva's Visual Suite in 2022" [V-open: silicon.co.uk copy]).
- Low price compared with Adobe; for teams, a flat price "to have up to five people at no extra cost" (see EM).

### HOW (resources, activities, partners)
- Browser-based editor and real-time collaboration; huge content library (templates, stock photos via Pixabay and Pexels acquired 2019; Smartmockups and Kaleido 2021; Flourish data visualisation, Feb 2022) (Wikipedia [V-open], level C; Kaleido as foundation of later AI work: Cam Adams in TechCrunch 29 Jul 2024 [V-open: https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/29/canva-acquires-leonardo-ai-to-boost-its-generative-ai-efforts/, accessed 2026-11-07]).
- Creator/contributor community supplying templates and assets against royalties [M for 2022; the later Creator Fund is verified, see CV-04].
- Product-led growth with very large engineering and content teams (about 3,200 staff in Oct 2022, Print21 [V-open: https://print21.com.au/?p=1122, accessed 2026-11-07], weak source).
- Partners: print fulfilment, integrations; no developer platform yet (Apps SDK only in June 2023, see CV-03).

### ECONOMIC MODEL (revenue model, cost structure)
- **Freemium:** a free tier for the mass base; revenue from a small paying minority (11m paying users vs 100m MAU in Oct 2022 per Print21 [V-open], weak; to be confirmed).
- **Pro:** per-person subscription for individuals. Expected about US$119.99/year or US$12.99/month in the US in 2022 [M]; **must be confirmed on the Wayback snapshot of 9 Jun 2022**.
- **Teams (Canva for Teams):** flat price for a group of up to five people, the key t0 feature. Verified retrospectively: legacy US five-person plans paid "$119.99 per year", and in Australia "the flat $39.99 AUS ... per-month fee for up to five users" (TechCrunch 3 Sep 2024 [V-open: https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/03/canva-has-increased-prices-for-its-teams-product/, accessed 2026-11-07]); Canva said "Our original pricing reflected the early stage of this product and has remained unchanged for the last four years" (same article), i.e. since 2020. Other sources quote US$149.90/year for the first five people [V-snippet only]. PricingSaaS captured "$300/year flat rate for first 5 people with volume tiering" on 1 Jan 2024 [V-open: https://pricingsaas.com/companies/canva/diffs/2024Q1, accessed 2026-11-07]. The exact mid-2022 figure is therefore open.
- **Enterprise:** in 2022 the 2019 team-oriented "Enterprise" tier was being folded into Canva for Teams (SmartCompany and a blog, [V-snippet only]); a large-organisation Enterprise plan (from US$300 per seat per year) existed only from May 2024 (TechCrunch 23 May 2024 [V-open]).
- **Other revenue:** print products, content [M].
- **Cost structure:** development-based, highly scalable software (fixed costs for engineering and content, low marginal cost per free user), consistent with years of positive free cash flow; statutory revenue US$962m in 2022 with statutory losses largely due to share-based pay (Startup Daily 6 May 2026 [V-open: https://www.startupdaily.net/other/canva-smacked-with-792000-in-fines-for-lodging-fy24-financials-11-months-late/, accessed 2026-11-07]; Capital Brief 22 Sep 2025 [V-open]).

### Canva

**History in brief.** Canva was founded in Australia in 2012 by Melanie Perkins, Cliff Obrecht and Cameron Adams and launched its browser-based editor in 2013 (Canva press release, 10 Apr 2025). It grew by making design accessible to people without training: templates replaced the blank page and drag-and-drop replaced pixel editing (Canva press release, 16 Apr 2026). It moved from individuals to small teams in 2019-2020 and to large organisations only in 2024, when it launched a "proper" Enterprise plan (TechCrunch, 23 May 2024). The company is private and headquartered in Sydney. It reports over 265 million monthly active users, over 31 million paying users and US$4 billion of annual recurring revenue at the end of 2025 (TechCrunch, 18 Feb 2026). Accounts lodged with the Australian regulator ASIC show statutory revenue of US$962 million in 2022 and US$2.1 billion in 2024, with statutory losses driven largely by share-based pay, while Canva describes itself as free-cash-flow profitable (Startup Daily, 6 May 2026).

**Business model before AI (mid-2022).** *WHO:* a mass base of non-designers (individuals, small businesses, educators, marketers) reached mostly through search and word of mouth, with small teams as the main paying segment and enterprises still marginal; the 2019 "enterprise" product was in practice a team product (TechCrunch, 23 May 2024). Canva passed 100 million monthly users in October 2022 (Business Wire, 12 Oct 2022; to be verified). *WHAT:* fast, easy design of social graphics, presentations, documents and print products, without skills and at low cost. *HOW:* a web editor with real-time collaboration, a very large library of templates and stock content (Pixabay and Pexels acquired in 2019), and product-led growth. *ECONOMIC MODEL:* freemium, with a per-person Pro subscription and a Teams plan sold at a flat price for up to five people, a price that Canva later described as "unchanged for the last four years" (TechCrunch, 3 Sep 2024). The cost structure was that of classic, highly scalable software: serving a free user cost little. (Exact 2022 list prices to be confirmed on the Wayback snapshot of 9 June 2022.)

**AI moves.** In the generative phase (2022-2024) Canva added single-step features: Magic Write (December 2022) and the Magic Studio suite with the Canva Shield trust framework and a US$200 million creator compensation fund (October 2023). It bought Affinity (about US$380 million) and the generative-AI start-up Leonardo.ai, which was kept running independently (TechCrunch, 29 Jul 2024). It also repriced Teams from a flat price to a per-seat price, citing its AI features, and partly reversed the increase for legacy customers after a backlash (TechCrunch, 3 Sep 2024; MarTech, 14 Oct 2024).

In the agentic phase (2025-2026) the moves accelerate. Visual Suite 2.0 introduced Canva AI as a conversational assistant, Canva Code and Canva Sheets (Canva press release, 10 Apr 2025). In June 2025 Canva became the "first design connector" for ChatGPT and launched an MCP server that lets AI assistants access a user's Canva workspace (Canva press release, 26 Jun 2025); Claude followed in July 2025 (Engadget, 14 Jul 2025), and Canva was a launch partner of apps in ChatGPT in October 2025. In October 2025 the "Creative Operating System" added a proprietary Canva Design Model, Canva Grow for marketing, a new Business plan and a free Affinity (Canva press release, 30 Oct 2025). In early 2026 Canva brought Brand Kits into Claude and ChatGPT and claimed more than 12 million designs created or accessed via AI assistants (IT Brief, 6 Feb 2026). In April 2026 it acquired Simtheory and Ortto and launched Canva AI 2.0, an agent with orchestration, memory, connectors and scheduled tasks, released as a research preview (Canva press release, 16 Apr 2026). At the same time Canva quantified AI usage per plan and launched an AI Pass add-on (PricingSaaS, Apr 2026).

The cost of serving AI then became visible. In its Q2 2026 shareholder update Canva cut its 2026 growth target from 30% to 20%, calling it "a deliberate decision to get the economics right", because it had been "relying too heavily on frontier models" (Startup Daily, 5 Aug 2026; Fortune, 12 Aug 2026). It raised the Pro price from US$144 to US$180 and reversed it six weeks later, while cutting free AI from 200 to 20 uses (PricingSaaS, Aug-Sep 2026). In October 2026 it released two in-house models, Craft for images and Brush for design editing, both presented as faster and cheaper (TechCrunch, 22 Oct 2026; Startup Daily, 3 Nov 2026), and in November it cut 22% of its workforce to "refound" the company (OfficeChai, 4 Nov 2026).

**Strategies identified (first version).** Canva combines five strategies strongly. (1) An *own agent* inside the editor, built on proprietary models whose purpose is both editability and cost. (2) Being *used by external agents* as a distribution channel: Canva treats LLMs as the new search engine and positions its editor as the "final mile" where AI drafts are finished (Obrecht, TechCrunch, 16 Apr 2026); company claims on referral traffic are contradicted by Similarweb data, so the size of this channel is uncertain. (4) Serving *proprietary context* (Brand Kits, design history, memory) to its own and external agents, while restricting brand features on the free plan. (5) *Cost-driven hybrid monetization*: the seat remains the anchor, AI becomes a metered allowance with an add-on, and free AI is rationed; the frequent reversals show that the model is not yet stable. (6) *Expanding scope* toward code, data and above all marketing execution through Canva Grow and a series of acquisitions. Acquisitions of AI-native firms (7) are frequent but small; AI capabilities are integrated, while acquired products with their own users run in parallel. Hosting third-party agents (3) is weak, and outcome-based pricing was not observed. What has not changed is the freemium core, the seat-based subscription and the role of the editable artifact inside Canva. The working interpretation, to be tested in the analysis, is that Canva defends its complementary assets (user base, templates, brand kits, the editor) by opening them to agents, while the inference cost of a huge free base forces it to evolve its economic model.
