## B. Brief history (about 230 words)

Figma was started by Dylan Field and Evan Wallace, who began experimenting with web-based design tools while studying at Brown University and were funded through a Thiel fellowship (S29, author bio); the company was incorporated in October 2012 (S1). The first product, Figma Design, launched in 2016 as a browser-based, multiplayer interface design tool (S14). Plugins arrived in 2019, FigJam (an online whiteboard) in 2021, and in 2022 Figma opened offices in Paris, Berlin and Tokyo and let Community creators monetize their resources (S1). On 15 September 2022 Adobe agreed to buy Figma for about USD 20 billion (S12); after fifteen months of regulatory review the deal was abandoned on 18 December 2023 (S38), and Figma received a USD 1.0 billion termination fee (S1). In June 2023 Figma launched Dev Mode for developers (S36) and acquired the AI design start-up Diagram (S35); FigJam AI followed in November 2023 (S37), and Figma AI plus Figma Slides in June 2024 (S34). In 2025 Figma doubled its product portfolio with Figma Make, Sites, Buzz and Draw (S1, S28), restructured its seats (S14), listed on the NYSE on 31 July 2025 at USD 33 per share (S1) and acquired Weavy, now Figma Weave (S27). Revenue passed USD 1 billion in 2025 (S4). In 2026 Figma enforced AI credit limits, opened its canvas to external agents, launched its own Figma agent and moved code onto the canvas (S5, S18, S29, S9). [All V-open]

## C. Business model in 2022 (t0)

Framework terms follow the course (Parolini 2021): WHO = target market, WHAT = value proposition, HOW = firm structure and network, ECONOMIC MODEL = how value is captured and the cost logic.

By mid-2023 "on our paid plans more developers visit Figma than designers". Cost logic: a development-based, highly scalable model (almost no variable cost per use). | S12, S36 [V-open]; prices [V-snippet], must be checked on the Wayback snapshot below |

## F. Draft section for the setup document (English, about 990 words)

### Figma

**History in brief.** Figma was founded by Dylan Field and Evan Wallace and incorporated in 2012 (Figma prospectus, 2025). Its first product, Figma Design, launched in 2016 as a browser-based interface design tool in which several people could work on the same file at once (Figma blog, 2024). FigJam, an online whiteboard, followed in 2021 (Figma prospectus, 2025). In September 2022 Adobe agreed to acquire Figma for about USD 20 billion (Adobe, 2022); the deal was abandoned in December 2023 for lack of a path to regulatory approval (Figma blog, 2023). Figma listed on the New York Stock Exchange in July 2025 at USD 33 per share and passed USD 1 billion in annual revenue in 2025 (Figma prospectus, 2025; Figma press release, 2026). It is the pilot case of this thesis because, as a listed company, it publishes the most detailed evidence on how AI changes revenue and costs.

**Business model before AI (mid-2022).** *Who:* product designers and the cross-functional teams that build digital products with them (developers, product managers, writers), from students and freelancers on free plans to large enterprises; Adobe described "millions of designers and developers and a loyal student following" (Adobe, 2022). *What:* a single, shared, browser-based canvas for designing and prototyping interfaces, with design systems and libraries as the team's source of truth, plus FigJam for ideation and a Community of files and plugins. *How:* a web-first multiplayer architecture, product-led growth through self-service on figma.com combined with a direct sales team, and an extensible platform built on public APIs and more than 10,000 plugins and widgets (Figma prospectus, 2025). *Economic model:* freemium subscription billed per paid editor seat on Professional, Organization and Enterprise plans, with free viewers (Figma blog, 2023; list prices to be confirmed on archived pricing pages). The model was highly scalable: Figma was expected to exit 2022 above USD 400 million ARR with net dollar retention above 150% and gross margins of about 90% (Adobe, 2022).

**AI moves.** In the generative phase Figma moved cautiously. It acquired the AI design start-up Diagram in June 2023 (Figma blog, 2023), added AI to FigJam in November 2023 (Figma blog, 2023) and launched Figma AI in June 2024 as a free beta built on "third-party, out-of-the-box AI models", warning that usage limits might follow once costs were known (Figma blog, 2024). The agentic phase was faster and broader. In March 2025 seats were restructured into Full, Dev, Collab and View, with a higher Full seat price (Figma blog, 2024). Config 2025 introduced Figma Make (prompt to app), Sites, Buzz and Draw (Figma blog, 2025). In June 2025 Figma released an MCP server that gives coding agents such as Claude Code and Cursor access to design context (Figma blog, 2025), and in October 2025 it launched an app in ChatGPT (Figma blog, 2025) and acquired Weavy, now Figma Weave (Figma blog, 2025). In March 2026 three things happened together: Figma began selling AI credit add-ons, enforced the monthly credit limits attached to every seat, and opened its canvas to external agents, which can now create and edit designs with the team's components and variables; writing is free in beta and "will eventually be a usage-based paid feature" (Figma blog, 2025; Figma blog, 2026). Its own Figma agent was opened to all users at Config in June 2026 and became generally available, consuming credits, on 6 October 2026 (Figma help center, 2026). In October 2026 Figma also put working code on the canvas with code layers (Figma press release, 2026).

The economic effects are visible in the filings. Cost of revenue grew 117% in Q2 2026 while revenue grew 48%, and non-GAAP gross margin fell from 92% in 2024 to 82% in Q3 2026, mainly because of AI infrastructure and hosting costs (Figma 10-Q, 2026; Figma press releases, 2025-2026). At the same time AI use is broad: in October 2026, 65% of customers above USD 10,000 ARR used the Figma agent weekly and over 80% consumed AI credits (Figma press release, 2026).

**Strategies identified (first version).** Figma combines five strategies strongly. (1) It is *used by external agents* under seat-anchored terms: writing requires a paid Full seat, access is rate-limited by plan, and usage-based pricing is announced; Figma reports that large customers using its MCP server grew Full seats about 70% faster (Figma press release, 2026). (2) It *serves proprietary context*: design systems, skills and the new Specs format make the customer's accumulated design knowledge usable by agents, which fits the complementary-assets argument (Teece, 1986). (3) It applies *cost-driven hybrid monetization*: the seat remains the anchor, credits act as a cost cap, extra usage is sold in pooled subscriptions or pay-as-you-go, and credit prices reflect "the model's cost to Figma" as well as value (Figma help center, 2026). (4) It *builds its own agent*, increasingly on a first-party model to differentiate and lower cost (Figma press release, 2026). (5) It *expands its scope* toward code and production (Make, code layers, the Payload backend), web publishing, marketing assets and generative media. *Acquisitions of AI-native firms* are present but small and integrated (Diagram, Weavy, the Bud team), and *hosting third-party agents* is weak: Figma hosts external tools and models, not an agent store. Outcome-based pricing is absent; governance appears as an Enterprise add-on with AI hosting controls (Figma help center, 2026).

What has not changed matters as much: Figma still sells per-seat subscriptions on the same four plans, keeps a free plan and free viewers, and explains growth mainly by new paid customers and seat expansion (Figma 10-Q, 2026). Its own filings name the risk that agents "could reduce the number of paid seats required for a given level of output" and, since May 2026, that AI model providers may "offer competing products" and "intermediate end-user relationships", a concern sharpened by Anthropic's Claude Design in April 2026 (Figma 10-Q, 2026; Anthropic, 2026). Figma's answer, so far, is to keep the canvas at the centre: open to every agent, but on its own terms.
