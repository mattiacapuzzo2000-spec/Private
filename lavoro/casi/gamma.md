## Draft for the setup document (English, about 290 words)

### Gamma as an AI-native benchmark

Gamma shows what a new entrant looks like when entry barriers fall. Founded in late 2020, it launched a card-based presentation tool in 2022; the turning point was March 2023, when "Gamma AI" began generating whole decks from a prompt with GPT-4 [TechCrunch 2025; Product Hunt]. It relies on third-party models (Google, Recraft and Flux image models are listed in 2026) [Gamma changelog]. With about USD 90 million raised and roughly 50 employees, it reported USD 100 million of ARR, profitability and 70 million users at its November 2025 Series B (USD 2.1 billion valuation), and "more than 100 million" users by October 2026: founder claims, not audited figures [TechCrunch 2025, 2026].

Its business model was AI-native from the start: freemium with AI credits (formalized in September 2025, with an Ultra tier), an API (general availability November 2025), connectors in Claude (January 2026), ChatGPT (March 2026) and Slack (July 2026), and a full agent with Gamma 5 (October 2026) [Gamma developer changelog]. Where incumbents charge for generation, Gamma increasingly charges for the agent's work: agent jobs are "priced by the work the agent does", while connectors, MCP and API keys are open on every plan, Free included [Gamma developer docs]. In September 2026 its pricing page began advertising "unlimited AI creations", agent and API still on credits, yet its help center still describes credits per slide and image: to be verified [PricingSaaS; Gamma Help Center].

Gamma matters because it attacks the incumbents' territory from below: with Gamma Imagine (March 2026) it moved into marketing graphics "to better compete with the likes of Canva and Adobe", targeting the "underserved" middle between professional tools and PowerPoint [TechCrunch 2026]. It is the clearest example of the supervisor's point: AI lowers the cost of building a creation tool, so incumbents must defend with assets an entrant cannot quickly copy.
