# 1. Topic and research question

## 1.1 Context: falling entry barriers and agentic AI

Between 2023 and 2026 generative AI lowered the barriers to entry in software. Deloitte observes that as "it becomes easier and easier for anyone to write code through generative AI tools, the cost of producing code approaches zero" (Deloitte, 2025), and IDC expects the traditional barriers to entry in application markets to be "increasingly challenged" by software designed as agents (Newmark, 2025). Vibe-coding platforms show the speed of the change: Lovable reported USD 400 million of annual recurring revenue (ARR) in February 2026 with 146 employees (TechCrunch, 2026a).

Creation tools are exposed from three directions. First, AI-native entrants: Gamma, a presentation tool, reported USD 100 million of ARR and 70 million users in November 2025 with about 50 employees (TechCrunch, 2025). Second, the model labs and big-tech suites now build creation products themselves: Anthropic launched Claude Design in April 2026 and Google launched Pics inside Workspace in September 2026 (Anthropic, 2026; Google, 2026). Third, general assistants produce documents, slides and images directly from a chat (Anthropic, 2025; Microsoft, 2025). On top of generative AI, which produces content on request, agentic AI adds systems that plan, use tools and execute multi-step tasks (Sapkota et al., 2026), including operating other companies' software.

For incumbents that spent a decade building creation software, the strategic question is how to defend the value they have built, react, and evolve their business model in this changed competitive context. What fell most is the barrier to producing a first artifact. The other barriers remain: distribution, installed base, file formats, template and asset libraries, brand kits and design systems. These are the complementary assets (Teece, 1986) that incumbents still control, and much of their response can be read as an attempt to turn them into reasons for users, and agents, to stay with them.

## 1.2 The puzzle

Practitioner frameworks give incumbents contrasting advice. For workflows that agents can easily penetrate but not fully automate, Bain recommends that incumbents "launch their own agents fast, deepen partner integrations to raise switching costs, and limit access to critical end points". For workflows that agents can both penetrate and automate, it asks them to "pick a lane: either become the neutral agent platform or supply the unique data that powers it", and it warns of "becoming a silent back end while the semantic gatekeepers harvest the margin" (Crawford et al., 2025, pp. 24-28). IDC, by contrast, argues that "if an agent cannot find your capability, your capability does not exist" (Newmark, 2026).

Creation incumbents did not wait for the debate to settle. Between 2024 and 2026 all four cases in this study opened their products to external agents (ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and coding agents through the Model Context Protocol, MCP) while building agents of their own. Opening is therefore not the question; the terms are. The thesis asks what these companies opened, on which terms, what they kept for themselves, and how this is changing their business models.

## 1.3 Research question

**Research question.** *How are creation-oriented productivity SaaS incumbents evolving their business models as agentic AI enables digital artifacts to be produced by AI agents, both inside their products and through external agents that use them?*

- **SQ1.** Which roles do these companies take toward AI agents (own agents inside the product, tool for external agents, host of third-party agents), under which access terms, and when?
- **SQ2.** How do these roles change the who, what, how and economics of the business model between 2022 and 2026, to what depth, and what remains unchanged?
- **SQ3.** How do these companies capture value and cover the cost of AI when an agent, rather than a human user, produces the artifact?

The question refines the one behind the deposited title ("How can productivity SaaS companies evolve their business models in response to the emergence of agentic AI?"). It is formulated empirically, "how are ... evolving", so that the strategies emerge from observed cases rather than from speculation; the prescriptive question, "how can", is answered in the managerial implications. The thesis covers both phases of the technology: generative AI (2023-2024) and agentic AI (2025-2026), so that it can also show how much each phase changed.

## 1.4 Population

*Creation-oriented productivity SaaS* are defined as cloud-delivered software products (Mell & Grance, 2011) whose primary value proposition is to enable users to create, edit or transform digital artifacts, such as graphics, presentations, documents, interface designs, videos or websites, that retain value outside the software itself, and that serve primarily horizontal, cross-industry markets. This is an operational definition for the thesis, not an established category in the literature. Four criteria must all be met:

- the product is delivered as SaaS and used across industries;
- the company describes the product with a creation verb (create, design, write, edit, build) applied to an artifact;
- the artifact can be exported or published;
- the product is an incumbent, with a documented commercial business model in mid-2022, before the launch of ChatGPT.

Excluded are task-coordination tools (Trello, Asana, monday.com), systems of record (Salesforce, HubSpot), communication tools (Slack, Zoom), storage (Dropbox, Box) and suites in which the creation product cannot be isolated (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, used only as context). Boundary cases such as Notion, Wix and Miro are discussed but not used as cases. A boundary test on about 20 products taken from commercial software categories will document the choice (Chapter 1).

## 1.5 Framework and method

The cases are analysed with the framework of the course: the entrepreneurial formula, or business idea, made of the target market (to whom?), the value proposition (what?) and the firm structure (how?), closed by the economics of the model (Parolini, 2021). The framework belongs to the Italian tradition of the *formula imprenditoriale* (Coda, 1984/2012), which draws on Normann's (1977) business idea, and maps onto the Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder & Pigneur, 2010). Following the course principle that what matters is the fit among the components, an AI feature that changes a single component is coded as a product change, while linked changes in at least two components that require a new fit are coded as business model change, in line with Foss and Saebi (2017).

The course distinction between artisanal, industrial and development-based activities is also central. Classic SaaS is development-based and highly scalable: serving one more user costs almost nothing. Generative and agentic AI add a variable cost to every use (inference), which pushes part of the model back toward a less scalable cost structure (Shapiro & Varian, 1999). This helps to explain AI credits, caps on free AI usage and several price changes observed in the cases.

The method is an exploratory multiple-case study (Eisenhardt, 1989; Yin, 2018) based on document analysis (Bowen, 2009). Sources are pricing, help and developer pages, current and archived; company announcements; filings and earnings calls where available; executive interviews and quality press; and academic and practitioner literature, combined following multivocal review guidelines (Garousi et al., 2019). Each case is coded at three points in time: mid-2022 (before ChatGPT), December 2024 (end of the generative phase) and 30 November 2026 (data cut-off). Every move is recorded with its date, source, status (announced, beta, generally available, monetized) and level of agency, together with what did not change. The design does not require interviews; three to five validation interviews with SaaS product or pricing managers could be added if useful.

## 3.2 Figma

**History in brief.** Figma was founded by Dylan Field and Evan Wallace and incorporated in 2012 (Figma prospectus, 2025). Its first product, Figma Design, launched in 2016 as a browser-based interface design tool in which several people could work on the same file at once (Figma blog, 2024). FigJam, an online whiteboard, followed in 2021 (Figma prospectus, 2025). In September 2022 Adobe agreed to acquire Figma for about USD 20 billion (Adobe, 2022); the deal was abandoned in December 2023 for lack of a path to regulatory approval (Figma blog, 2023). Figma listed on the New York Stock Exchange in July 2025 at USD 33 per share and passed USD 1 billion in annual revenue in 2025 (Figma prospectus, 2025; Figma press release, 2026). It is the pilot case of this thesis because, as a listed company, it publishes the most detailed evidence on how AI changes revenue and costs.

**Business model before AI (mid-2022).** *Who:* product designers and the cross-functional teams that build digital products with them (developers, product managers, writers), from students and freelancers on free plans to large enterprises; Adobe described "millions of designers and developers and a loyal student following" (Adobe, 2022). *What:* a single, shared, browser-based canvas for designing and prototyping interfaces, with design systems and libraries as the team's source of truth, plus FigJam for ideation and a Community of files and plugins. *How:* a web-first multiplayer architecture, product-led growth through self-service combined with a direct sales team, and an extensible platform built on public APIs and more than 10,000 plugins and widgets (Figma prospectus, 2025). *Economics:* freemium subscription billed per paid editor seat on Professional, Organization and Enterprise plans, with free viewers (Figma blog, 2023; list prices to be confirmed on archived pricing pages). The model was highly scalable: Figma was expected to exit 2022 above USD 400 million ARR with net dollar retention above 150% and gross margins of about 90% (Adobe, 2022).

**AI moves.** In the generative phase Figma moved cautiously. It acquired the AI design start-up Diagram in June 2023, added AI to FigJam in November 2023 and launched Figma AI in June 2024 as a free beta built on "third-party, out-of-the-box AI models", warning that usage limits might follow once costs were known (Figma blog, 2023, 2024). The agentic phase was faster and broader. In March 2025 seats were restructured into Full, Dev, Collab and View, with a higher Full seat price (Figma blog, 2024). Config 2025 introduced Figma Make (prompt to app), Sites, Buzz and Draw (Figma blog, 2025). In June 2025 Figma released an MCP server that gives coding agents such as Claude Code and Cursor access to design context, and in October 2025 it launched an app in ChatGPT and acquired Weavy, now Figma Weave (Figma blog, 2025). In March 2026 three things happened together: Figma began selling AI credit add-ons, enforced the monthly credit limits attached to every seat, and opened its canvas to external agents, which can now create and edit designs with the team's components and variables; writing is free in beta and "will eventually be a usage-based paid feature" (Figma blog, 2026). Its own Figma agent was opened to all users at Config in June 2026 and became generally available, consuming credits, on 6 October 2026 (Figma help center, 2026). In October 2026 Figma also put working code on the canvas with code layers (Figma press release, 2026).

The economic effects are visible in the filings. Cost of revenue grew 117% in Q2 2026 while revenue grew 48%, and non-GAAP gross margin fell from 92% in 2024 to 82% in Q3 2026, mainly because of AI infrastructure and hosting costs (Figma 10-Q, 2026; Figma press releases, 2025-2026). At the same time AI use is broad: in October 2026, 65% of customers above USD 10,000 ARR used the Figma agent weekly and over 80% consumed AI credits (Figma press release, 2026).

**Strategies identified (first version).** Figma combines five strategies strongly. (1) It is *used by external agents* under seat-anchored terms: writing requires a paid Full seat, access is rate-limited by plan, and usage-based pricing is announced; Figma reports that large customers using its MCP server grew Full seats about 70% faster (Figma press release, 2026). (2) It *serves proprietary context*: design systems, skills and the new Specs format make the customer's accumulated design knowledge usable by agents, which fits the complementary-assets argument (Teece, 1986). (3) It applies *cost-driven hybrid monetization*: the seat remains the anchor, credits act as a cost cap, extra usage is sold in pooled subscriptions or pay-as-you-go, and credit prices reflect "the model's cost to Figma" as well as value (Figma help center, 2026). (4) It *builds its own agent*, increasingly on a first-party model to differentiate and lower cost (Figma press release, 2026). (5) It *expands its scope* toward code and production (Make, code layers), web publishing, marketing assets and generative media. *Acquisitions of AI-native firms* are present but small and integrated (Diagram, Weavy, the Bud team), and *hosting third-party agents* is weak: Figma hosts external tools and models, not an agent store. Outcome-based pricing is absent; governance appears as an Enterprise add-on with AI hosting controls (Figma help center, 2026).

What has not changed matters as much: Figma still sells per-seat subscriptions on the same four plans, keeps a free plan and free viewers, and explains growth mainly by new paid customers and seat expansion (Figma 10-Q, 2026). Its own filings name the risk that agents "could reduce the number of paid seats required for a given level of output" and, since May 2026, that AI model providers may "offer competing products" and "intermediate end-user relationships", a concern sharpened by Anthropic's Claude Design in April 2026 (Figma 10-Q, 2026; Anthropic, 2026). Figma's answer, so far, is to keep the canvas at the centre: open to every agent, but on its own terms.

## 3.3 Canva

**History in brief.** Canva was founded in Australia in 2012 by Melanie Perkins, Cliff Obrecht and Cameron Adams and launched its browser-based editor in 2013 (Canva press release, 2025a). It grew by making design accessible to people without training: templates replaced the blank page and drag-and-drop replaced pixel editing (Canva press release, 2026). It moved from individuals to small teams in 2019-2020 and to large organisations only in 2024, when it launched a full Enterprise plan (TechCrunch, 2024a). The company is private and headquartered in Sydney. It reports over 265 million monthly active users, over 31 million paying users and USD 4 billion of ARR at the end of 2025 (TechCrunch, 2026b). Accounts lodged with the Australian regulator ASIC show statutory revenue of USD 962 million in 2022 and USD 2.1 billion in 2024, with statutory losses driven largely by share-based pay, while Canva describes itself as free-cash-flow profitable (Startup Daily, 2026a).

**Business model before AI (mid-2022).** *Who:* a mass base of non-designers (individuals, small businesses, educators, marketers) reached mostly through search and word of mouth, with small teams as the main paying segment and enterprises still marginal (TechCrunch, 2024a). Canva passed 100 million monthly users in October 2022 (Canva press release, 2022; to be verified). *What:* fast, easy design of social graphics, presentations, documents and print products, without skills and at low cost. *How:* a web editor with real-time collaboration, a very large library of templates and stock content (Pixabay and Pexels were acquired in 2019) and product-led growth. *Economics:* freemium, with a per-person Pro subscription and a Teams plan sold at a flat price for up to five people, a price that Canva later described as "unchanged for the last four years" (TechCrunch, 2024b). The cost structure was that of classic, highly scalable software: serving a free user cost little. Exact 2022 list prices are to be confirmed on archived pages.

**AI moves.** In the generative phase (2022-2024) Canva added single-step features: Magic Write (December 2022) and the Magic Studio suite with the Canva Shield trust framework and a USD 200 million creator compensation fund (October 2023). It bought Affinity (about USD 380 million) and the generative-AI start-up Leonardo.ai, which was kept running independently (TechCrunch, 2024c). It also repriced Teams from a flat price to a per-seat price, citing its AI features, and partly reversed the increase for legacy customers after a backlash (TechCrunch, 2024b; MarTech, 2024).

In the agentic phase (2025-2026) the moves accelerated. Visual Suite 2.0 introduced Canva AI as a conversational assistant, Canva Code and Canva Sheets (Canva press release, 2025a). In June 2025 Canva became the "first design connector" for ChatGPT and launched an MCP server that lets AI assistants access a user's Canva workspace (Canva press release, 2025b); Claude followed in July 2025 (Engadget, 2025), and Canva was a launch partner of apps in ChatGPT in October 2025. In October 2025 the "Creative Operating System" added a proprietary Canva Design Model, Canva Grow for marketing, a new Business plan and a free Affinity (Canva press release, 2025c). In early 2026 Canva brought Brand Kits into Claude and ChatGPT and reported more than 12 million designs created or accessed via AI assistants (Canva press release, 2026). In April 2026 it acquired Simtheory and Ortto and launched Canva AI 2.0, an agent with orchestration, memory, connectors and scheduled tasks, released as a research preview (Canva press release, 2026). At the same time Canva quantified AI usage per plan and launched an AI Pass add-on (PricingSaaS, 2026).

The cost of serving AI then became visible. In its Q2 2026 shareholder update Canva cut its 2026 growth target from 30% to 20%, calling it "a deliberate decision to get the economics right", because it had been "relying too heavily on frontier models" (Startup Daily, 2026b; Fortune, 2026). It raised the Pro price from USD 144 to USD 180 a year and reversed the increase six weeks later, while cutting free AI from 200 to 20 uses (PricingSaaS, 2026). In October 2026 it released two in-house models, Craft for images and Brush for design editing, both presented as faster and cheaper (TechCrunch, 2026c; Startup Daily, 2026c), and in November it cut 22% of its workforce to "refound" the company (OfficeChai, 2026).

**Strategies identified (first version).** Canva combines five strategies strongly. (1) An *own agent* inside the editor, built on proprietary models whose purpose is both editability and cost. (2) Being *used by external agents* as a distribution channel: Canva treats AI assistants as the new search engine and positions its editor as the "final mile" where AI drafts are finished (TechCrunch, 2026d); company claims on referral traffic are contradicted by third-party traffic data, so the size of this channel is uncertain. (3) Serving *proprietary context* (Brand Kits, design history, memory) to its own and to external agents, while restricting brand features on the free plan. (4) *Cost-driven hybrid monetization*: the seat remains the anchor, AI becomes a metered allowance with an add-on, and free AI is rationed; the frequent reversals show that the model is not yet stable. (5) *Expanding scope* toward code, data and above all marketing execution, through Canva Grow and a series of acquisitions. *Acquisitions of AI-native firms* are frequent but small: AI capabilities are integrated, while acquired products with their own users run in parallel. *Hosting third-party agents* is weak, and outcome-based pricing was not observed. What has not changed is the freemium core, the seat-based subscription and the role of the editable artifact inside Canva. The working interpretation, to be tested in the analysis, is that Canva defends its complementary assets (user base, templates, brand kits, the editor) by opening them to agents, while the inference cost of a huge free base forces it to evolve its economic model.

## 3.4 Adobe Express

**Background.** Adobe Express is Adobe's entry-level, template-first content creation app. It descends from Adobe Spark (2016), was relaunched in December 2021 as Creative Cloud Express and was renamed Adobe Express during 2022 (TechCrunch, 2021; Adobe blog, 2022a). It is analysed as an embedded case: the unit of analysis is the product, while Adobe is context and company-level moves are recorded only when they touch Express. This choice has a cost: Adobe does not report Express revenue, and its indicators mix Express with Acrobat ("Acrobat plus Express" monthly active users, over 900 million in Q3 FY2026) and with Firefly and mobile apps (Adobe earnings call, 2026c).

**Business model before AI (2022).** *Who:* non-professional creators, from students to small business owners, described by Adobe as people "focused on outcome and not process", plus over 43 million K-12 users of the free education version and Creative Cloud members (TechCrunch, 2021, 2022). *What:* quick creation of social posts, flyers, short videos and web pages from templates, with Adobe Stock images, Adobe Fonts and simplified Photoshop and PDF functions; "AI" meant template and font recommendations. *How:* the product repackaged technologies and content libraries that Adobe already owned, and was distributed through the web, mobile app stores, the Creative Cloud base, schools and partners embedding it, such as Wix from October 2022 (Adobe blog, 2022b). *Economics:* freemium, with Premium at USD 9.99 per month and inclusion in Creative Cloud plans; a development-based, highly scalable cost structure without per-use costs (TechCrunch, 2021). In this segment Express is the challenger: Canva is the leader, even if Adobe's own communication never names it.

**Generative phase (2023-2024).** Adobe rebuilt the product as an "AI-first" editor integrated with its Firefly models (general availability in August 2023), stressing that Firefly was "designed to be safe for commercial use" (Adobe press release, 2023). In September 2023 Adobe introduced generative credits across Creative Cloud, Firefly and Express, including monthly credits for free users, and raised Creative Cloud prices citing "new costs associated with generative AI", while leaving Express prices unchanged (Adobe blog, 2023). In April 2024 Express became one of the first creation tools inside an AI assistant, as a custom GPT in OpenAI's GPT Store that searched Express templates and sent the user back to the Express app (Adobe blog, 2024).

**Agentic phase (2025-2026).** Two channels developed at very different speeds. Inside the product, the AI Assistant announced at Adobe MAX in October 2025 lets users create and edit designs through conversation, orchestrating first- and third-party models; its agency is multi-step with user confirmation. Adobe announced that at general availability it would be offered to all users through Firefly credits and credit packs (Adobe press release, 2025b), but in October 2026 it was still in beta, on desktop only and not for Teams, Enterprise or Education plans (Adobe help center, 2026; Adobe Community, 2026b). Outside the product, Express moved fast: an Express agent for Microsoft 365 Copilot (announced in March 2025, live in 2026), apps in ChatGPT declared "free to ChatGPT users globally" (December 2025), a connector in Claude (April 2026), a unified Adobe plugin in ChatGPT (August 2026), Slack (September 2026) and Gemini on all plans (September 2026) (Adobe press release, 2025a, 2025c; Adobe Community, 2026a; Adobe blog, 2026a, 2026b, 2026c, 2026d). The access terms are precise: anyone can start, often as a guest, but signing in with an Adobe account "unlocks generative capabilities", higher usage limits and saving across sessions (Adobe blog, 2026a, 2026b, 2026d). Users are invited to continue "in Adobe Express" for finer edits.

**Economics today.** Management describes this as a freemium funnel. In June 2026 Adobe said it would shift visitors from direct-to-paid journeys to friction-free onboarding "at the cost of short-term ARR", and that the monetization model for its agents "is basically about credit consumption", with upsell toward Firefly and Creative Cloud (Adobe earnings call, 2026b). The seat price of Premium has not moved since 2021; the meter sits on generation: free users get a daily allotment, Premium users monthly credits, and editing actions in the assistant do not consume free generations (Adobe Community, 2026b). Packaging, not price, is where Express changed: complimentary Premium was withdrawn from some Acrobat and Photography plans in 2025, and Express Premium was folded into the paid Acrobat Studio bundle (Adobe Community, 2025; Business Wire, 2025; Adobe press release, 2026).

**Strategies identified (first version).** The dominant strategy is *being used by external agents*, under "free entry, metered generation" terms, combined with *cost-driven hybrid monetization*: the seat as anchor, credits as a cost cap, rationed free AI. The *own agent* is present but still in beta. *Hosting third parties* exists only as a pre-agentic platform (add-ons, an embed SDK, an MCP server for developers), with no third-party agents inside Express. *Proprietary context* is weak: brand styles are mentioned, but no brand kit served to external agents was found. *Scope expansion* takes an unusual form: rather than growing, Express is being turned into a creation layer that Acrobat, Adobe's own agents and external assistants call (Adobe press release, 2026; Adobe earnings call, 2025). No acquisition targeted Express.

**Why this profile.** Three drivers explain it. First, the challenger position: Express has no large paid seat base to protect, so distribution inside assistants costs little in cannibalization and reaches users where their intent starts. Second, Express belongs to a portfolio that monetizes elsewhere: Adobe measures it by monthly active users and converts usage into credits, Firefly plans and Creative Cloud or Acrobat Studio upgrades (Adobe earnings call, 2026a, 2026b). Third, cost structure: a free base of this size makes inference a real variable cost, which explains credits from 2023 and the slow general availability of the in-product agent. In Teece's (1986) terms, Adobe brings its complementary assets (templates, Stock, Photoshop-grade tools, a "commercially safe" model) into other companies' assistants instead of protecting them behind its own interface.

**What has not changed, and limits.** The freemium structure, the USD 9.99 Premium price, the inclusion in Creative Cloud, the template-first logic and the target segment are the same as in 2022. Evidence on results is weak: Express-specific figures are qualitative statements or growth rates from earnings calls (for example, Express monthly active users tripled year on year in Q1 FY2026; Adobe earnings call, 2026a), and Adobe MAX (10-12 November 2026) may change the picture before the data cut-off.

## 3.5 Grammarly (now part of Superhuman)

**History in brief.** Grammarly was founded in 2009 by Max Lytvyn, Alex Shevchenko and Dmytro Lider, three Ukrainian entrepreneurs, as a grammar checker for students (Grammarly, 2026). It later moved its headquarters to San Francisco and kept a large engineering base in Kyiv. Its distinctive choice was to work on top of other applications rather than build its own editor: by November 2021 it served 30 million people every day across more than 500,000 apps and websites, was profitable and cash-flow positive, and raised over USD 200 million at a USD 13 billion valuation (Grammarly, 2021; TechCrunch, 2025b). Since 2023 the company has changed more than any other case in this study. In December 2024 it agreed to acquire the document platform Coda, whose co-founder Shishir Mehrotra became CEO (Grammarly, 2024). In 2025 it raised USD 1 billion of revenue-based financing from General Catalyst, bought the email client Superhuman and, on 29 October 2025, renamed the whole company Superhuman, with Grammarly becoming one product in a four-product suite (Grammarly, 2025a, 2025b, 2025c).

**Business model before AI (2022).** *Who:* English-language writers, from students to professionals and non-native speakers, reached through a large free tier; teams through Grammarly Business; schools through Grammarly for Education; and, from 2021, developers through a text editor SDK (Grammarly, 2021, 2022). *What:* real-time, explainable feedback on correctness, clarity, tone and consistency, inside the user's own applications, with brand and style controls for teams. *How:* proprietary natural-language-processing models and a distribution infrastructure of browser extensions, desktop apps, mobile keyboards and integrations that place Grammarly in more than 500,000 apps. Grammarly transforms the text but does not host it: the email stays in Gmail, the document in Word. *Economics:* freemium subscription, with an individual Premium plan, per-seat Business plans and institutional contracts; "we make money from subscriptions, not from selling data or ads" (Grammarly, 2021). Before large language models the marginal cost of a suggestion was low, so the model was highly scalable. The 2022 price points still have to be checked on archived pricing pages.

**AI moves.** In the generative phase Grammarly added GrammarlyGO (March 2023), which composes, rewrites and drafts replies in any app, and immediately rationed it with monthly prompt caps: 100 prompts for Free users and 500 for Premium at launch (Grammarly, 2023; 9to5Google, 2023). In July 2023 it announced the closure of its developer SDK, effective January 2024, to focus on the core product, AI and enterprise customers (TechCrunch, 2023). In 2024 it merged Premium and Business into a single Pro plan at USD 12 per member per month billed annually, with 2,000 prompts, and launched Authorship, a tool that shows which parts of a text were written by a person or by AI (Grammarly support, 2026).

In the agentic phase the moves accelerated. In August 2025 Grammarly launched specialized "agents" (Proofreader, Reader Reactions, AI Grader, Citation Finder, AI Detector and others) in a new writing surface, at no extra cost (Grammarly, 2025d). At the October 2025 rebrand it launched Superhuman Go, a proactive assistant built into the Grammarly extension that coordinates first- and third-party agents across apps, an Agent Store with partner agents, and an Agents SDK in closed beta (Grammarly, 2025c). Partners such as Box, Gamma and Wayground followed in February 2026 (Superhuman, 2026a). In 2026 the company bought Rows, GPTZero and Fathom, turned Coda into Superhuman Docs with an MCP server that lets ChatGPT and Claude read and write documents on paid plans, and released Go 1.0, in which users build custom agents that run on schedules or events (Superhuman, 2026a, 2026b, 2026c, 2026d, 2026e). In May 2026 Grammarly Enterprise started to be sold only "through Superhuman Go" (Grammarly enterprise page, 2026). One AI feature, "Expert Review", which attributed AI feedback to real writers without consent, was withdrawn after a class action (TechCrunch, 2026e).

**Strategies identified (first version).** Grammarly's profile differs from the other cases. Its strongest strategies are *hosting third-party agents*, *expanding scope* into a work suite and *acquisitions*, supported by an *own agent* whose autonomy grew from single-step generation to triggered custom agents. These choices share one logic: the asset that general-purpose assistants cannot easily copy is not Grammarly's text generation but its position at the user's cursor in a million applications, and the company turns that overlay into a platform on which its own agents, partner agents and acquired products run. This fits the complementary-assets lens (Teece, 1986): the incumbent defends what it built, distribution and daily habit, rather than the feature that AI commoditized. The financing structure matters too: capital repaid through a share of revenue, raised explicitly for sales, marketing and acquisitions, favors bundles and upsell. *Being used by external agents* is weak for Grammarly itself: no official Grammarly connector for ChatGPT or Claude was found, and the SDK was closed; opening happens only for the suite products that host artifacts (Docs, Mail), on paid plans. *Cost-driven monetization* is present in a light form: the seat price has not moved, while AI use is capped by prompts and soft limits, and usage-based pricing for agents is announced (Superhuman, 2026d). *Proprietary context* is present through style guides, brand tones and acquired context sources (documents, email, meetings). Outcome-based pricing is absent. Governance, through Authorship, AI detection and the GPTZero acquisition, appears to be a deliberate lever, especially in education. What has not changed is also telling: Grammarly is still sold on its own, with the same free tier and the same USD 144 annual price as Premium in 2022.

## 3.6 Benchmark: Gamma, an AI-native entrant

Gamma shows what a new entrant looks like when entry barriers fall. Founded in late 2020, it launched a card-based presentation tool in 2022; the turning point was March 2023, when "Gamma AI" began generating whole decks from a prompt with GPT-4 (TechCrunch, 2025). It relies on third-party models (Google, Recraft and Flux image models are listed in 2026). With about USD 90 million raised and roughly 50 employees, it reported USD 100 million of ARR, profitability and 70 million users at its November 2025 Series B (USD 2.1 billion valuation), and "more than 100 million" users by October 2026: founder claims, not audited figures (TechCrunch, 2025, 2026f).

Its business model was AI-native from the start: freemium with AI credits (formalized in September 2025, with an Ultra tier), an API (general availability in November 2025), connectors in Claude (January 2026), ChatGPT (March 2026) and Slack (July 2026), and a full agent with Gamma 5 (October 2026) (Gamma developer changelog, 2026). Where incumbents charge for generation, Gamma increasingly charges for the agent's work: agent jobs are "priced by the work the agent does", while connectors, MCP and API keys are open on every plan, Free included (Gamma developer documentation, 2026). In September 2026 its pricing page began advertising "unlimited AI creations", with the agent and the API still on credits, yet its help center still describes credits per slide and image; this remains to be verified.

Gamma matters because it attacks the incumbents' territory from below: with Gamma Imagine (March 2026) it moved into marketing graphics "to better compete with the likes of Canva and Adobe", targeting the middle ground between professional tools and PowerPoint (TechCrunch, 2026f). It is the clearest example of the point raised in our call: AI lowers the cost of building a creation tool, so incumbents must defend themselves with assets that an entrant cannot quickly copy.

# 4. First observations across the cases

These observations are preliminary and will be tested in the full analysis.

- **Opening to agents is universal; the terms differ.** All four incumbents opened to external agents between 2024 and 2026, under very different conditions: free entry with metered generation (Adobe Express), features reserved for paid plans (Canva), write access tied to a paid seat with announced usage-based pricing (Figma), and opening limited to the suite products that host artifacts (Grammarly).
- **The economics changed most, and the driver is cost.** Figma's non-GAAP gross margin fell from 92% (2024) to 82% (Q3 2026), mainly because of AI infrastructure; Canva cut its 2026 growth target from 30% to 20% "to get the economics right". Pricing is cost-based, with credits acting as caps and the seat as anchor; no outcome-based pricing was observed, against the advice of several consultancies.
- **Own agents reach general availability late.** The Figma agent became generally available in October 2026; Canva AI 2.0 is a research preview and the Adobe Express assistant is still in beta. The agentic phase is therefore visible mostly in moves and terms, less in results.
- **Complementary assets explain much of the defence:** design systems (Figma), templates and brand kits (Canva), stock content and professional tools inside a portfolio (Adobe), and position at the user's cursor in a million applications (Grammarly).
- **The core has not disappeared.** Freemium tiers and seat-based subscriptions persist in all four cases; the changes are layered on top of them.
